# Published continuations for a minor-showing 1♠

Researched 2026-10-27. Scope: Cranberry Moscito's 11–16 unbalanced
1♠, showing a six-card-or-longer minor or both minors at least 5–4,
with a four-card major possible beside a six-card minor. The working
definitions are in [Openings](../src/Openings.md) and
[Minor Multi 1♠](../src/1S.md).

There is a particularly close published example from an actual partnership:
Normand Houle–Stéphane Turcotte. There are also detailed MOSCITO continuations
that address the same bidding-space problem. None of the sources below
documents every aspect of Cranberry's opening and responses unchanged.

## Closest match: Houle–Turcotte, 2023

Houle reports playing this with Stéphane Turcotte: 1♠=11–14, six-card minor
or minors with a major fragment; 5–5 minors without a fragment open 2NT.
[First-person article, 28 July 2023](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/strong-club-for-system-geeks/).

Responses: 1NT enquiry; 2♣ pass/correct; 2♦/♥ major transfers (completion
needs xx+); 2♠ long-minor invitation/tolerance for other; 2NT weak/GF
support for both minors; 3♣ invitational support for both minors.
[Houle's 29 July comment](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/strong-club-for-system-geeks/?cj=1380760).

| After 1♠–1NT | Opener's description |
|---|---|
| 2♣ / 2♦ | 6+ clubs/diamonds |
| 2♥ / 2♠ | Three cards in that major, minors 5–4+ |
| 2NT | Six clubs, four diamonds |
| 3♣ | Six diamonds, four clubs |

Unspecified: enquiry strength, later relays, transfer rejection,
four-card-major handling.

Matthew Haag reports similar UK play: game-forcing 1NT, pass/correct 2♣;
weak 2♣ majors, Multi 2♦, Muiderberg 2M. Complete continuations are absent.
[Haag's first-person comment](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/strong-club-for-system-geeks/?cj=1380995).

## Detailed tournament precedent: Marston–Thomson, 2005

The pair's Bermuda Bowl submission gives 1♠=10–14, 6+ diamonds or minors
5–4+; club one-suiters open 2♣. This near match guarantees diamonds.
[Tournament submission, PDF pages 2 and 15; notes page 13](https://info.ecatsbridge.com/Systems/2005WorldChampionships-Estoril/BermudaBowl/australia/marston-thomson.pdf).

| Response to 1♠ | Treatment |
|---|---|
| 1NT | Relay, 11+; includes some invitations |
| 2♣ / 2♦ | Show 5+ hearts/spades |
| 2♥ | Show 5+ clubs |
| 2♠ | Invite in opener's longer minor; responder has 3+ in each |
| 2NT | Diamond invitation |
| 3♣ | Pass/correct to opener's longer minor |
| 3♦ | Natural, approximately 6–9 |
| 3♥ / 3♠ | Natural, nonforcing |

Over major-showing responses, the intervening step denies Hx. Responder's
3♣ seeks minor preference; 2NT continues. Then natural rebids show minima;
the other major/3NT show maxima with clubs/diamonds. The PDF includes
shape and honour relays after 1NT.

## Published weak-1NT alternative: J-Moscito, 2004

Jari Böling allocates weak hands to 1NT and the strong enquiry to 2♣.
His 1♠ guarantees diamonds; single-suited clubs open 2♣.
[Author's notes, §§4.2 and 5.5, PDF pages 6–8 and 19–20](https://users.abo.fi/jboling/bridge/j_moscito.pdf).

| Response to 1♠ | Treatment |
|---|---|
| 1NT | Forcing; 0–11 or certain game-forcing splinters |
| 2♣ | Relay with game interest, 12+ |
| 2♦ | Four-card-major enquiry, 8–11 |
| 2♥ / 2♠ | Natural, 5+ cards, 8–11 |

After 1NT, opener bids 2♣ with 4+ clubs, otherwise 2♦ with six diamonds.
Responder can stop in either minor or show weak hearts; other rebids
handle invitations and splinters. The author acknowledges losing 1NT.

This is an author's design; tournament use was not independently verified.

## A further allocation: MOSCITO 2007

The archived 2007 notes reserve 2♣ for 5+ spades and 4+ hearts;
2♦/2♥ show five-card-or-longer hearts/spades. The 1NT enquiry requires
11+. A simple major completion denies three-card support; 2NT and 3M
separate better and weaker raises. After 1♠–2♦–2♥, responder's 2♠ shows
five hearts and four spades. Three clubs is minor preference, while
2NT continues with game interest.
[MOSCITO 2007, §2.1.4, page 1](https://relaysystems.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/moscito-2007.pdf).

Again, club one-suiters open 2♣. This document is useful as a continuation
reference; it does not identify a partnership on its title page.

## What can transfer to Cranberry

The following are design inferences, not additional published agreements:

- **Houle is the closest starting point for the opening itself.** Both
  minor one-suiters fit into 1♠ and 2♣ remains available as a weak opening.
  His response allocation uses 2♣ for a cheap minor escape, however,
  whereas Cranberry spends it on spades.
- **The strong relay can coexist with major-showing responses and direct
  nonforcing majors.** Marston–Thomson put the latter at the three level.
  That does not establish a solution preserving Cranberry's direct 2M
  signoffs as well as two major transfers and a weak minor escape.
- **A weak 1NT route has precedent.** J-Moscito makes the immediate rebids
  2♣/2♦ available for weak responder to pass, but pays for this by moving
  the strong relay to 2♣. Cranberry's all-strength enquiry is a further
  adaptation, not the same published treatment.
- **Both-major hands deserve their own explicit continuations.** The
  2007 allocation gives 5♠4+♥ the lowest suit response. This supports
  investigating Cranberry's low spade transfer, without establishing
  that its particular 2♣/2♦ arrangement has already been published.
- **Keep the opening differences visible when borrowing.** Diamond
  raises and splinters in older MOSCITO depend on known diamond length;
  that inference disappears when a club one-suiter can open 1♠.

## Source and verification notes

These are original system notes, a tournament submission, and first-person
player reports. No claim is made that the agreements remain in use today.
The Bridge Winners continuations are in comments rather than the article
body; the site's public comments feed was used to verify the linked
comments when the text-only page reader omitted them. The relevant
Marston–Thomson PDF pages were visually checked because its extracted suit
symbols are corrupted.
