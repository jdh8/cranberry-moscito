# Three-card and four-card support after transfer major openings

Researched 2026-10-27. Scope: uncontested continuations after **1♦ showing
4+ hearts** and **1♥ showing 4+ spades**, principally Moscito variants.
Below, **M** means the major shown by opener, not the denomination of the
opening bid; **m** means a minor. INV = invitational; GF = game-forcing;
NF = nonforcing. Strengths belong to the cited version.

The central distinction is whether responder **shows the support length
immediately**, or **starts an enquiry and shows support later**. Published
Moscito methods use both. A three-card raise can deliberately accept a
seven-card fit; it does not necessarily promise that opener has five.

## Explicit immediate separation: Moscito 2007

The [hosting archive](https://relaysystems.wordpress.com/symmetric-relay-systems/)
identifies this document as Paul Marston's notes.
These 9–14 transfer openings are unbalanced and exclude 4M–6m.

| Support | Strength / type | After 1♦ | After 1♥ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exactly three | 6–8 | 2♥ | 2♠ |
| Exactly three | 9–11 | 2♦ | 2♥ |
| Exactly four | 6–9 | 3♥ | 3♠ |
| Exactly four | 9–11 | 2NT | 2NT |
| Exactly four | 13–15, balanced | 3NT | 3NT |

The table does not assign a route to very weak four-card raises.
The spade 2NT raise usually lacks shortage. After the constructive
three-card response, opener's 2NT tries for game with 5M–4m; 3♣/3♦ shows
a maximum with 4M–5m. The other major is a long-suit game try; 3M is NF;
splinters are available.

The immediate step, 1♥/1♠, includes an ordinary notrump response,
major/notrump game tries, and any 13+ hand. Therefore it is not uniformly
INV+. [System notes, §§1, 2.1.2–2.1.3 and 3.3–3.4, pages 1 and 3](https://relaysystems.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/moscito-2007.pdf).

## Delayed separation: Jari Böling's J-Moscito, 2004

The 9–14 openings exclude 4333, 4432 and 5332. Responses:

| Holding | Route |
|---|---|
| Three-card support, 0–6 | May use semi-forcing 1NT |
| 3+ support, 7–11 | Direct 2M, constructive |
| 4+ support, preemptive | Direct 3M |
| INV+ | 1♥ over 1♦; 1♠ over 1♥: 12+, or 11+ with fit |
| Exactly three, 15–16, 4333-like | Direct 3NT |

Here 2NT forces 3♣ for a weak minor signoff, other-major mini-splinter,
or strong void-splinter.

[Author's notes, revised 8 October 2004, §§2 and 4.1, pages 3–5](https://users.abo.fi/jboling/bridge/j_moscito.pdf#page=5).

After one relay and opener's two-suit disclosure, a **simple major raise
shows three**, a **jump raise four-plus**; both are invitational. Examples:

| Auction so far | Three-card invitation | Four-plus invitation |
|---|---|---|
| 1♦–1♥–1♠ (hearts/clubs) | 2♥ | 3♥ |
| 1♥–1♠–1NT (spades/clubs) | 2♠ | 3♠ |

Continuing the relay normally establishes GF; the notes expressly except
1♦–1♥–1♠–2♣. The invitation rule applies to the specified two-suit
branches, not every possible rebid. [§§5.4 and 5.4.2, pages 15 and 18–19](https://users.abo.fi/jboling/bridge/j_moscito.pdf#page=19).

## Four-card limit raises and delayed side-suit support: Marston, 2005

Paul Marston's July 2005 first draft uses roughly 9–14 openings, including
13–14 balanced hands with a four-card major. Direct 2NT shows a
four-trump limit raise; 3M shows four-card support and about 6–9. Direct
3NT is a balanced game raise, about 12–15; that paragraph does not specify
its support length. Opener's new suit after 2NT/3NT shows shortage.
[Author's introduction, §§1.1–1.3 and 2.4, pages 2 and 4](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/moscito2005intro.pdf#page=4).

The first step includes balanced invitations and any game-going hand.
A second step establishes GF; a non-step 2M or 2NT is invitational.

Natural two-over-one responses are approximately 8–11 and force one round,
allowing delayed support. The example
**1♥–2♦–2♥–2♠** uses responder's three spades and five diamonds.
Thus support can be shown after a side suit rather than immediately.
[§§2.1–2.2, pages 2–3](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/moscito2005intro.pdf#page=3).

## Finding opener's fifth card: Marston–Thomson, 2005

The Estoril partnership card specifies 10–14 transfer openings and a
2NT limit raise without specifying its trump length. Its detailed shape
enquiries distinguish four from five cards explicitly.

After either **1♦–1♥–1NT–2♣** or **1♥–1♠–1NT–2♣**:

| Opener's next bid | Shape information |
|---|---|
| 2♦ | Four in the shown major, five diamonds |
| 2♥ | Balanced, exactly four in the shown major |
| 2♠ | Five in the shown major, 5332 |

Other responses describe three-suiters. The balanced branches are maximum
because lower balanced hands use other openings. These are continuations
of an enquiry, not direct support responses. The guidelines send any
GF 13+ through relays, plus approximately 11–12 balanced without a
five-card suit, later breaking out in notrump or raising NF.
[Partnership card and notes dated 23 August 2005, PDF pages 2–3, 12 and 14; printed notes pages 1, 10 and 12](https://info.ecatsbridge.com/Systems/2005WorldChampionships-Estoril/BermudaBowl/australia/marston-thomson.pdf#page=12).

Do not silently combine this partnership's agreements with the separately
published July draft.

## Firsthand variants confirm that there is no single Moscito raise scheme

Richard Willey, posting as hrothgar in 2016, describes 1♥ as 4+ spades,
roughly 9–14. Direct 2♠ is a three-card value raise; 3♠ is a four-card
value raise; 2NT is a limit-or-better four-plus raise. Immediate
3♣/3♦/3♥ shows six cards there and three spades, NF. He explicitly accepts
playing a seven-card major fit even when a longer minor fit exists.
[Firsthand account, post 26, 14 February 2016](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/73552-canape-with-precision/page__st__20#entry877090).

Kungsgeten's 2013 hybrid excludes 4333/4432 from these openings. Over
1♦, the 1♥ relay includes an 8–10 three-card raise; 2♦ combines an
11–13 three-card raise with an 8–10 four-card raise; 2♥ shows 0–7 with
3+ support; 2NT is INV+ with 4+. The post does not fully specify how
subsequent bidding resolves the mixed 2♦ response.
[Firsthand account, post 2, 4 June 2013](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/60528-moscito-sequences/#entry729673).

## Implications for Cranberry

These are design deductions, not further published agreements.

- **Decide what the low-level raise tells opener.** Current
  [1♦](../src/1D.md) and [1♥](../src/1H.md) continuations give both the
  constructive artificial raise and the preemptive 2M response 3+ support.
  Consequently neither identifies whether the partnership has seven or
  eight trumps when opener has four. If that information is the priority,
  give four-card hands a separate route or specify a later separation.
- **A three-card raise needs an agreed escape or acceptance policy.**
  Opener with four cards must know whether to accept the seven-card fit,
  suggest notrump, or show a longer minor. Raising does not itself discover
  the eighth trump. This matters especially when the major can be weaker
  or shorter than opener's minor.
- **Keep the enquiry available for uncertain game choice.** Responder
  with three trumps and enough strength to investigate can learn whether
  opener has four or five before choosing the major game or notrump.
  A four-card raise already guarantees an eight-card fit and can spend its
  remaining space on strength, shortage and slam suitability.
- **Set point ranges for Cranberry independently.** Its
  [transfer openings](../src/Openings.md) are 11–16, stronger than these
  historical variants. Copying their response point ranges would change
  how light Cranberry invites and forces to game.
- **Check shape and forcing assumptions too.** Cranberry's transfer
  definitions do not exclude balanced hands. An imported continuation
  must accommodate them. Its natural NF 1NT also does not ensure another
  turn to show weak three-card support; importing J-Moscito's route would
  require changing that agreement.
- **Resolve the branch allocation before copying continuations.** The
  current 2NT is Lebensohl, 3M invitational, and 3NT a slam try. Adopting
  another version's raises would displace those meanings. Start with the
  three-versus-four-card distinction, then fit the strength bands and
  enquiries around the chosen response map. A delayed invitation split
  is especially worth considering if retaining the existing 2NT allocation.

## Verification and limits

These sources document historical partnership agreements, not their
comparative playing strength or present-day prevalence. Relevant PDF
tables were rendered and visually checked because extracted suit symbols
were unreliable, especially in the 2005 material. Page numbers above
distinguish physical PDF pages from the partnership notes' numbering.
The forum examples are firsthand descriptions, not complete manuals.

No bidding-system source file was changed by this research.
