# Swedish Club continuations with five-card-major responses

Researched 2026-10-27. Scope: continuations after Cranberry Moscito's
1♣ opening, currently 12–14 balanced or 17+; the strong balanced branch
starts at 18 because 15–17 balanced opens 1NT. See
[Openings](../src/Openings.md). Following the user's clarification, this
research assumes limited hands with four-card majors use the existing
transfer openings, removing four-card majors from the weak 1♣ branch. The user's
priority is therefore **finding opener's three-card support opposite
responder's five-card major**, before investigating four–four fits.
The user accepts **8+ HCP** as the threshold for these immediate major
responses; weaker five-card majors can start with 1♦.
Other opening bids are outside this research.

There is a firsthand published example with **both major responses
guaranteeing five cards**. It is a useful starting point, but its author's
later clarification excludes some weak balanced hands from 1♣. The more
complete mainstream Swedish Club notes found use four-card-major responses.

## Closest response allocation: WellSpyder, 2012

The author reports playing 1♣=11–13 balanced or 16+.
[Firsthand description, post 37, 13 March 2012](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/51458-2-way-club-system/page__st__20#entry620434).

| Response | Published meaning |
|---|---|
| 1♦ | 0–7, or 8–11 balanced |
| 1♥ | 8+, 5+ spades |
| 1♠ | 8+, 5+ hearts |
| 1NT | 8+, 5+ clubs |
| 2♣ | 8+, 5+ diamonds |
| 2♦ | 14+ balanced |
| 2♥ / 2♠ | Approximately 4–7, six cards |
| 2NT | 12–13 balanced, invitational |

Weak opener completes the suit-showing response, or jumps with good fit;
other rebids show 16+ and use Precision-style asks. Exact strong rebids,
forcing limits and 4441 responses are not supplied.

## Four-card majors: the same player's later clarification

In 2016, WellSpyder specifies that weak 1♣ **always has a four-card major**.
After 1♣–1♦, opener's 1M shows either 11–13 with four cards or 16–19
with five. The 1♦ response includes 8–11 balanced hands, allowing
four–four fits to emerge; weak opener can pass responder's 1NT rebid.
[Firsthand clarification, posts 6 and 8, 25–26 May 2016](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/74248-a-precisionswedish-club-hybrid/).

This differs directly from Cranberry: the weak club branches have opposite
four-card-major requirements. The published negative continuations cannot
simply transfer to Cranberry. Four–four fits need not drive Cranberry's
weak-branch design; they remain relevant when opener is strong.

## Detailed mainstream reference: Erik Sjöstrand, 2014

Sjöstrand's notes use 11–13 balanced or 17+. Immediate 1♥/1♠ show **4+**
cards and 8+ HCP. Negative 1♦ includes most 0–7 hands and flat 8–11
hands without a four-card major. Thus merely changing 1M to promise five
would leave some four-card-major hands without their documented route.
[Author's notes, §§1–4](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/swedishclub.pdf).

Over 1M, weak opener uses 1NT, a simple raise, or 1♠ over hearts;
strong hands have artificial 2♣ and 2♦ treatments and natural rebids.
The negative branch includes a multi-purpose forcing 1♥ rebid rather than
an automatic weak 1NT. The document supplies considerably more continuation
detail, but its major-response definition does not meet the five-card
preference unchanged. [Same notes, §§3–4](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/swedishclub.pdf).

## Detailed relay alternative: Suffolk Swede / Spadelet

Chris Chambers publishes detailed 1♣ relay notes with a shape-restricted
weak branch: 11–13 balanced/semi-balanced, exactly two spades. The 1♥
response includes both five-spade positives and 8–13 balanced hands, so
it does **not** guarantee five spades. Heart positives are divided between
1NT and 2♣ by secondary shape. Weak opener rebids 1NT after 1♥ and
2♥ after the heart responses; strong opener uses the next-step relay.
[Author's notes, pages 1–3](https://dubiouslogic.com/bridge/system/swede/1C-GMS.pdf),
[partnership card, page 2](https://dubiouslogic.com/bridge/system/swede/system-cards/Spadelet_WBF.pdf).

This is useful relay material, but neither its opening restriction nor its
mixed spade response matches Cranberry's priority unchanged. Some later
branches in the notes remain explicitly unresolved.

## A historical distinction: strong Carrot versus two-way Carrot

Flodqvist's original strong-club module has 1♠=8+ with five hearts and
1NT=8+ with five spades, both game-forcing. Its 1♥ response covers 8+
without a five-card major, six-card minor or 5–5 minors. This provides a
published home for many four-card-major hands, but only after an opening
that is already strong.
[Coauthor's description of Morotsklöver's 1♣, 2004](https://syskon.github.io/syskon/delsystem/003_morot_1c_01.htm).

The same author's later **two-way** Carrot description uses 10–12 balanced
or 17+ (normally 18+ balanced). Here 1M instead means 8+, **4+** cards,
forcing one round and game-forcing opposite the strong hand. Consequently,
the earlier five-card transfer structure is not evidence that the later
two-way club used it unchanged.
[Coauthor's Carrot Club description, version dated 29 August 2008](https://syskon.github.io/syskon/system/002_carrot_club_01.htm).

## Implications for a Cranberry continuation design

These are design deductions, not additional published agreements:

- **Start from the WellSpyder response allocation** if immediate five-card
  major information is the priority. With 1♥ showing spades, opener can
  complete at 1♠. With 1♠ showing hearts, completion reaches 2♥. This
  asymmetry deserves deliberate acceptance.
- **Make three-card support explicit.** A balanced weak opener without a
  four-card major has two or three cards in responder's major. Distinguishing
  those holdings directly answers the user's question. Automatic transfer
  completion, as published above, does not by itself make that distinction;
  a support-showing completion and a separate doubleton rebid would be a
  Cranberry adaptation requiring a full rebid table.
- **Keep the accepted 8+ HCP threshold.** Immediate major responses need
  only cover positive hands; weaker five-card majors can go through 1♦.
  With Cranberry's 17+ strong branch, an 8+ positive permits game-forcing
  continuations once opener identifies the strong hand.
- **Finish the negative branch before adopting the structure.** It includes
  weak hands and constructive balanced hands. A weak opener with no
  four-card major needs its own treatment; the later WellSpyder natural
  major rebids do not solve that case.
- **Defer four-card-major discovery when appropriate.** It is unnecessary
  opposite Cranberry's weak branch, but strong opener still needs methods
  for four–four fits, three-suited hands and responder's secondary suits.
- **Recalibrate responder's invitations.** Cranberry's weak range is a point
  higher than the closest example's. Copying every published strength band
  unchanged would be a separate design choice, not a verified match.
- **Keep strong balanced hands visible.** A positive response followed by
  a strong rebid must accommodate Cranberry's 18+ balanced hands as well
  as strong unbalanced hands; a vague reference to Precision asks is not
  a complete continuation table.

## Source and verification notes

The principal example is a player's firsthand forum report, not a full
system manual. Its 2012 response table was located through the public
search index and then verified against the original page's HTML; the
2016 clarification was also read directly. Relevant suit symbols in
Sjöstrand's PDF and the Spadelet card were checked visually. The Carrot
descriptions are by a system coauthor.
These establish published agreements, not present-day usage or comparative
playing strength.
